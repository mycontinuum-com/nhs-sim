GP Records's **Journal** shows dated contacts with their recorded narrative and authors. Search includes note text. Expand **Record details and activity** to inspect the history or open an action.

## Hospital documentation

Open a patient in Hospital EPR and choose **Documentation** or **New note**. Select a free-text, history-and-physical or progress-note template. The section navigator moves between parts of the note.

**Save draft** stores an editable note. **Review & sign** previews the note before **Confirm & sign** records the signature. Signed text is preserved. Use **Add addendum** to append further information with its own attribution. Drafts and signed notes remain in the documentation list after reload.

## Prescriptions and blood tests

**Prescription** opens the medication catalogue and order details. Enter the medicine, dose, unit, route, frequency, duration, quantity and indication. Review the details before sending the draft to pharmacy. Pharmacy review and approval are still required before dispensing.

**Request test** opens the laboratory catalogue. Choose a supported panel, specimen, priority, collection round and clinical details. Review and sign the request. Results become available after 120 simulation minutes for collection now, or 240 minutes for the next round.

The **Orders** view retains the submitted details. **Results** shows measurements against sample dates. Use the panel navigator to filter the grid, or select measurements and choose **Graph selected** to inspect trends. Values and reference intervals are synthetic.

## Agent requests

These workflows use `POST /api/sites/hospital/actions` with the team's API key. Patient records are read through `GET /api/sites/hospital/view?patient=SIM-000006`.

The **Secondary care** group in the <a href="/docs/explorer/">API explorer</a> also exposes raw records:

- `GET /api/sites/hospital/consultations?patient=SIM-000006` returns hospital-owned consultation records, including saved note sections, full text, signatures, and addenda where recorded.
- `GET /api/sites/hospital/genomes?patient=SIM-000006` returns the resident's raw synthetic genomic records.

Both require `hospital` scope and return `items`, `total`, `offset`, `limit`, and the simulation time `now`. Each item retains its resource ID, version, original `data`, and provenance. Use `limit` from 1 to 500 and increase `offset` to page through results. The default limit is 100. Omitting `patient` reads across the team's world. An unknown patient returns an empty list.

The consultation endpoint includes hospital-owned `hospital-note`, `clinical-note`, `consultation`, and `encounter` records. Their data shapes depend on the record kind. GP consultations and discharge-summary documents are separate records.

## Synthetic genomic records

Every synthetic patient receives a hospital-only CYP2C19 SNP panel for the clopidogrel integration scenario. The panel contains unphased genotype calls for `rs4244285`, `rs4986893`, and `rs12248560`. The SNP identifiers and reference alleles follow [PharmVar's CYP2C19 catalogue](https://www.pharmvar.org/gene/cyp2c19). Patient genotypes are deterministic synthetic fixtures, unrelated to real patients or population frequencies.

`data.variants` contains each SNP's identifier, reference allele, alternate allele, and diploid genotype. The panel labels itself as synthetic and identifies its source. It is a targeted SNP fixture, not a whole-genome sequence, inferred haplotype, or prescribing recommendation.

Existing populations and team-specific patients receive missing panels through a transactional startup migration. Repeated startup preserves existing records. New patients receive a panel when generated. Genome records remain owned by and visible to `hospital`; generic actions cannot edit or share them with another service. Operator access remains available for simulation administration.

The <a href="/api/openapi.json">OpenAPI document</a> describes the response schemas. The genome endpoint returns `genome-record` resources. The older `genomic-test` workflow stub is not a raw SNP record.

## Submit hospital actions

- Notes use `type: "hospital_note"` and `hospitalNoteCommand`. The command is `{kind: "save", template, sections}` for drafts, `{kind: "sign"}` for signing, or `{kind: "addendum", text}` for additions. Each section contains `id`, `heading` and `text`. Creation also requires `patientId` and `title`; edits require `resourceId` and `expectedVersion`.
- Prescriptions use `type: "draft_prescription"`, `patientId`, `title` and `medicationOrder`, containing `drug`, `dose`, `unit`, `route`, `frequency`, `duration`, `quantity` and `indication`.
- Laboratory requests use `type: "order_test"`, `patientId`, `title` and `bloodTestOrder`, containing `panel`, `panelId`, `specimen`, `priority`, `collection` and `clinicalDetails`. Supported panel IDs are `fbc`, `ue`, `hba1c`, `lft`, `crp` and `lipids`. Collection is `now` or `next-round`.

Supply an `Idempotency-Key` header or a `clientRequestId` UUID and reuse it for an identical retry. Stale edits and attempts to overwrite signed notes fail without changing the record.


## Deceased synthetic patients

Patient records include an optional `death` object with the date, authored cause, `synthetic: true`, and source `authored-synthetic-mortality-v1`. Its presence means the patient is deceased. GP and hospital records show the status, date, cause, and age at death. Existing consultation and medication history remains available.

The fixtures are `SIM-000009` (road traffic collision), `SIM-000011` (pneumonia), `SIM-000012` (sepsis), `SIM-000018` (cancer), and `SIM-000019` (stroke). Names, dates of birth, and ages can differ between populations. Read the current patient record for those values. These causes are authored simulation data.

The startup migration adds the fixtures to existing shared populations and patient overlays without changing demographics or replacing existing death records. New generated populations receive the same fixture IDs. Deceased patients are excluded from future generated demand and new telephone calls. Patient APIs retain the `death` object; PDS FHIR Patient responses include `deceasedDateTime`. FHIR `active` continues to describe record availability.
